# Personal phone audio

Join a driver normally. On a controller, open **Lobby / Ready → Enable Audio**. A keyboard driver can choose **Pair personal audio phone** or its **Audio QR** on the shared display, then open that link on a phone. The companion is portrait-friendly and adds no player. Tap **Connect / replace my audio device** and **Enable Audio**. A direct Enable Audio click also binds the authorized link. Replacing an endpoint explicitly fades/stops the old endpoint.

Tokens are random 192-bit values scoped to one existing participant, valid for five minutes, and omitted from room snapshots. Only that player's socket or the authenticated local display can issue the link. Reissuing invalidates old unused links; an already active endpoint continues until explicitly replaced. Leave, driver disconnect, or an empty room revokes that driver's audio pairing. A controller resume keeps its driver identity and can re-enable its integrated audio; a detached companion may need a fresh QR after the driver disconnects. Audio-device websocket reconnect works with its unexpired token. Host transfer does not change audio ownership.

`server-audio.js` sends version 1 `audio-frame` packets over the existing websocket, at 30 Hz: sequence, server timestamp, phase, elapsed, listener identity, at most four nearby emitters and bounded events. Emitters include world position, heading, velocity, RPM, load, gear, shift cut, slip and engine state. Events have unique room-scoped IDs, server timestamps, position, emitter, severity and radius. Car-to-car impacts emit one event, including both involved identities. No microphone, camera or PCM streaming is used.

The own engine has priority. Other cars attenuate with in-game 3D distance, become faint at 25 m, and are silent at 60 m. Bearing is computed in the driver's view; radial velocity produces Doppler clamped to ±10%. Paint and viewport order cannot change audio ownership. A four-engine worklet runs layered combustion harmonics, exhaust/noise texture, mechanical whine and class-specific turbo/hybrid layers. Data lives in `public/audio-presets.js`; firing rate is RPM/60 × cylinders/2. F1: six-cylinder hybrid; prototype: eight-cylinder turbo; drift: four-cylinder turbo/limiter; GT3: naturally aspirated six; stock: V8; wedge: naturally aspirated V12; hyper: turbo V8/hybrid; tourer: naturally aspirated V8. These are fictional configurations chosen consistently for these assets.

RPM and sequential/manual-style shift cues use a virtual automatic gearbox derived from authoritative speed and throttle. They do not claim a full engine/transmission simulation and do not change driving physics. Class-specific shift durations, limiter pulses and load modulation shape the character. Shift, turbo throttle-lift air release, drift lift crackle, collision, landing, lap, start and finish cues come from the authoritative simulation. Slip and wind are continuous telemetry layers. Crash loudness follows impact speed; repeated contact has a cooldown.

Web Audio runs only after a tap. AudioWorklet is preferred on secure contexts; otherwise cached original WAV engine loops use smoothed playback rates, gain and panning, plus tire/wind layers. The fallback is less detailed. Stereo panning falls back to mono when unavailable. The limiter/compressor and capped master gain bound output. Master, Own Engine, Opponents, Tires, Crashes, Ambient and SFX have independent sliders. Network gaps fade at 600 ms; old/duplicate events are discarded. A minimum observed clock offset estimates transport age without assuming synchronized phone clocks. This is responsive LAN playback, not sample-accurate synchronization or a promise of zero latency.

Personal sound is optional and the TV does not duplicate race engines by default. The computer's Audio settings panel can act as that keyboard driver's personal endpoint, subject to the same replacement rule. Mobile browsers may suspend audio when backgrounded or locked. Resume Audio is the recovery affordance; keep the page visible during play. Headphones improve stereo positioning.

Open `/audio-lab` for eight preset selection, RPM sweep, throttle/load, slip, shift and impact controls. Eight comparison WAVs include idle, acceleration, shift and lift. Tests verify distinct rendered samples, PCM headers, peaks/DC, asset hashes, distance/pan/Doppler, event deduplication and pairing security. Browser QA exercised decode, worklet initialization and simultaneous four-driver audio identity/status. No physical Android/iPhone listening, Bluetooth latency, actual gamepad hardware, or sustained mobile thermal/performance test was available. Those are unverified, not claimed as passing.

## Physical device check

1. On Android Chrome and iOS Safari, join different drivers, choose different cars/paints and tap Enable Audio. Confirm each page names the correct driver and levels affect only that phone.
2. Test Sound before racing. Start a solo race, then repeat with two, three and four drivers; verify viewport layout and audio ownership independently.
3. Drive cars together, apart and past one another. Check own engine priority, steering-relative pan, gentle Doppler, tire-slip changes and impact severity. Automated tests cover the exact 8/25/60 m attenuation scenario.
4. Disconnect/reconnect an audio phone, replace it explicitly, then leave the driver or transfer HOST. Confirm no added racer, duplicate crash, wrong identity or persistent loop. Regenerate an expired/revoked QR.
5. Lock/unlock and background/foreground each phone; confirm suspension is reported and Resume Audio recovers. Check device media volume, silent-mode behavior and Bluetooth delay. Test with speakers and headphones separately.
6. Run an extended four-player race and watch Audio diagnostics for late frames/events. Compare smoothness and device temperature; lower personal levels or use the sample fallback if needed. This checklist is provided for hardware validation and has not been marked as physically passed.

## Reference-inspired exhaust tone
The supplied Target effect video was inspected through frames; its separate M4A was measured for spectrum and level. It is bass-heavy (roughly 91–98% below 1 kHz in sampled driving windows). Direct listening was unavailable, so this is an interpretation, not a verified sonic match. No reference recording is copied into the game. All eight original voices now add phase-coherent crank/bank undertones, combustion saturation, a load-dependent two-pole exhaust filter and DC removal. Existing RPM/gear cuts and distinct cylinder/turbo/hybrid profiles remain. Rebuilt WAV loops and comparisons share the same DSP; the fallback also muffles its loop on throttle lift. Automated checks cover load/lift/cut behavior at 24 and 48 kHz and regenerated asset integrity. Refresh phone audio pages to load the change.


The realism revision replaces the dominant smooth tone with per-ignition pressure pulses, small seeded timing/pressure variations, a damped exhaust resonator, and load-driven intake texture. Turbo/hybrid/gear whine is quieter. RPM and load smoothing use time constants across playback sample rates. Fallback loops warm up before capture and trim the crossfade overlap to avoid a repeated non-adjacent seam. These remain procedural fictional engines; physical listening validation is outstanding.
